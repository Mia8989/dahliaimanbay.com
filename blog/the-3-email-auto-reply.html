The 3-Email Auto-Reply (How I Stopped Typing the Same Answer Five Times a Day)

Direct Answer

I open my Sent folder and find the three questions I answer most, usually pricing, hours, and location. I write one clean reply for each, turn on Templates in Gmail Settings, and save all three. Then I set up a filter for each one, so when an email contains a word like "pricing," Gmail sends that reply for me automatically. No app, no monthly fee, just Gmail doing what I used to do by hand.

I used to answer the exact same pricing email five times a day. Same words, different sender, same few minutes gone every single time. Somewhere around the fortieth time I typed "our rates start at" from memory, I stopped and asked myself why I was doing this by hand at all.

Why this is worth fixing

Every video in my "30 Hacks From Dahlia" series ends with the same line, and this is the hack that started the series, because it's the smallest fix with the most obvious payoff. Small business inboxes aren't full of interesting questions. They're full of the same three or four questions, over and over, from people who all deserve a fast, accurate answer and none of whom need me personally to type it.

The instinct is to treat every email like it needs a human moment. Most of them don't. A question with one true answer, the same answer every time, doesn't need my attention. It needs a reply that's already written, sitting there ready to go the second the right email lands.

The exact setup, step by step

Step 1: Find your three questions

Open your Sent folder and scroll. Don't guess at what you answer most, look at what you actually typed. For most small business owners it's some version of pricing, hours, and location, but yours might be different, maybe it's availability, or whether you ship internationally, or what your turnaround time is. Pick the three questions where the honest answer is the same every single time, no matter who's asking.

Step 2: Turn on Gmail Templates

Templates is a built-in Gmail feature, but it's off by default. Here's exactly where to find it.

  • Open Gmail and click the gear icon in the top right corner.
  • Click "See all settings."
  • Click the "Advanced" tab.
  • Find "Templates" and select "Enable."
  • Scroll to the bottom and click "Save Changes."

Step 3: Write and save your three replies

Now write the actual answers, one at a time, the way you'd want a stranger to read them.

  • Click "Compose" and write your reply exactly the way you want it sent every time.
  • Click the three-dot "More options" menu at the bottom of the compose window.
  • Hover over "Templates."
  • Under "Save draft as template," click "Save as new template."
  • Name it something you'll recognize later, like "Pricing reply."
  • Repeat for your other two questions, so you end up with three saved templates.

Step 4: Build the filter that sends them automatically

This is the part that turns a saved template into an actual auto-reply. You're telling Gmail which incoming words trigger which template.

  • Click the gear icon, then "See all settings."
  • Click "Filters and Blocked Addresses."
  • Click "Create a new filter."
  • In the "Has the words" field, type the words that tend to show up in that kind of question, for example pricing OR cost OR "how much."
  • Click "Create filter."
  • Check the box labeled "Send template," then choose the matching template from the dropdown.
  • Click "Create filter" again to save it.
  • Repeat the whole process for your other two questions and templates.
💡
Test it before you trust it

Send yourself a test email using the exact words you set the filter to catch, then check that the right template goes out and reads the way you want. Filters run silently in the background, so the only way to know it's working correctly is to watch it fire once on purpose.

How to pick your three questions

The Sent folder search is the fastest gut check, but the real test is simpler: does the honest answer change depending on who's asking, or is it always the same? Your hours are always the same. Your starting rate is usually the same. Where you're located is always the same. Those are safe to automate. If your real answer starts with "it depends," that question doesn't belong on this list, no matter how often it shows up.

Resist the urge to automate more than three to start. The value here isn't in covering every possible question, it's in clearing out the handful that are eating your time for no reason. Once the first three are running smoothly, you'll know exactly what a fourth one should be, if you even need one.

What not to automate

This hack works because it's narrow. The moment you try to stretch it to cover anything with nuance, judgment, or a quote attached, it stops helping and starts creating a different problem: a confident, well-formatted reply that's wrong for that particular person. A custom quote, a scheduling request, a complaint, a partnership pitch, anything where the right answer depends on details only a human would notice, none of that belongs in an auto-reply.

There's also a trust cost to getting this wrong. An auto-reply that answers the wrong question, or answers confidently when the honest answer is "it depends," reads worse than no reply at all. The whole point is that these three answers never change. If you catch yourself editing a template reply after it goes out, that's a sign the question wasn't as fixed as you thought, and it should go back to being answered by hand.

I didn't automate my inbox. I just stopped retyping the three answers that were already true every time.

Before you turn this on

✓
Start with three, not thirty. Pick the questions with one true answer that never changes, and leave everything else for you to answer by hand.
✓
Write the reply like a person, not a form letter. It's going out under your name, so read it back before you save it as a template.
✓
Test the filter with a real email first. Send yourself one using the exact trigger words before you trust it with a stranger's inbox.
✓
Revisit it when the true answer changes. If your rates or hours change, the template has to change with them, it won't update itself.

One hack, out of thirty

This is hack 1 in a series about the small, unglamorous fixes that quietly give a small business owner their day back. There's nothing clever about a Gmail filter, and that's exactly why it works: it's built in, it's free, and it's already sitting in Settings waiting to be turned on. A Gmail template is a saved block of email text you can drop into a new message or attach to a filter so Gmail sends it automatically. It is off by default. To turn it on, open Gmail, click the gear icon, choose See all settings, click the Advanced tab, find Templates, select Enable, then scroll down and click Save Changes.

Once Templates is turned on and you have saved a template, go to Settings, click Filters and Blocked Addresses, then Create a new filter. Enter the words that show up in that kind of question in the Has the words field, then click Create filter. Check the box labeled Send template, choose the template you want from the dropdown, and click Create filter again to save it. From then on, any incoming email matching those words gets that reply automatically.

No, not unless you tell it to. A new filter only applies to email that arrives after you create it. There is an optional checkbox, Also apply filter to matching conversations, that reaches back into your existing inbox. Leave it unchecked if you only want the auto-reply to apply going forward.

That is exactly why this hack is limited to three questions on purpose. Only automate the questions with one true answer that never changes by situation, like your hours or your starting rate. If a question needs judgment, a quote, or any back and forth, it should stay in your inbox for you to answer by hand.
