The 5-Answer Chatbot (So My Site Never Sleeps)

Direct Answer

I gave my site a simple chatbot loaded with the five questions I get asked most. It answers instantly, captures the visitor's email, and hands me a warm lead in the morning. This isn't a full AI system. It's a smart FAQ that never sleeps, and it starts with just five answers.

I kept losing people who visited my site at midnight, when there was nobody there to answer them. Someone would land on my homepage after hours, have a question, and leave without ever telling me they were there. No email, no message, no way to follow up. Every video in my "30 Hacks From Dahlia" series ends with the same line, and this hack is a good example of why: I help small teams do big things, and a lot of that is just closing the small gaps where you're quietly losing people.

Why after-hours visitors matter more than you think

If you run a small business or a nonprofit, your traffic doesn't stop when you go to bed. People research at night, on lunch breaks, on weekends, whenever it's convenient for them, not you. For a lot of small teams, there's nobody monitoring the site at 11pm to catch someone who has a question and is one unanswered question away from clicking away for good.

That's the gap I was losing people in. Not because my site was bad, but because it was static. It could show information, but it couldn't respond to the specific thing a visitor actually wanted to know in that moment. A chatbot that answers the five things people ask most closes that gap without needing a person behind it around the clock.

How to find your actual top five questions

The mistake I see most often is guessing. Someone sits down to build a chatbot and writes answers to the questions they assume people are asking, based on what they'd like people to ask, or what sounds impressive to answer. That's backwards. Your real top five questions are already sitting in your inbox and your sent folder.

Search your sent mail for the answers you find yourself typing over and over. If you've explained your pricing structure, your turnaround time, or how to book a consultation more than a handful of times in nearly the same words, that's a candidate for your chatbot. Do the same with your inbox: what do people keep asking before they'll commit to reaching out further? Those repeat questions, the ones you could answer in your sleep because you've answered them so many times, are exactly the five you want to load in first.

How to structure each answer

Once you have your five questions, write each answer the way you'd actually say it out loud to someone standing in front of you, not the way it reads in a formal FAQ page. Keep each answer short enough to read in a few seconds. If the honest answer to "how much does this cost" is "it depends," say that, then explain what it depends on and where to go next, rather than dodging the question.

Each answer should end somewhere. Not just a period, but a next step: a link to book a call, a prompt to leave an email for more detail, a way to keep the conversation going. A chatbot that answers a question and then goes quiet has done half the job. The other half is giving the visitor something to do next.

📧
Why the email matters more than the perfect answer

I'd rather have a decent answer plus an email address than a flawless answer and nothing else. A visitor who gets their question answered and leaves is gone the moment they close the tab. A visitor who leaves their email is a warm lead sitting in my inbox the next morning, someone I can actually follow up with. The chatbot's real job isn't to be exhaustive. It's to make sure the visit leaves a trace.

I didn't need my site to have all the answers. I needed it to catch the person before they left.

Not a full AI system, and that's the point

I want to be direct about what this hack is and isn't, because it's easy to overbuild it. This is not a full AI system that can hold an open-ended conversation, understand every possible question, or replace the judgment of a real person having a real conversation with a prospective client or donor. It's a smart FAQ that never sleeps. Five prewritten answers, an email field, and nothing more complicated than that.

That limitation is a feature, not a shortcoming. A five-answer chatbot is something you can build in an afternoon and actually maintain. A system that tries to answer anything a visitor might throw at it takes real work to build well, and even more work to keep accurate. Start with five answers. You can always expand later once you see what people are actually asking the chatbot itself.

Reviewing and updating your five answers

The five questions that matter most today won't necessarily be the same five in six months. Put a recurring check on your calendar, monthly or quarterly, whatever fits how fast your business or organization changes, and go back through your inbox and sent folder the same way you did the first time. If a new question has started showing up again and again, it belongs in the rotation. If one of your five answers is now out of date, whether that's a price, a policy, or a program that's changed, update it before it misleads someone.

This is a small maintenance habit, not a rebuild. It takes the same few minutes it took to find the questions the first time, and it keeps the chatbot honest instead of letting it quietly go stale while you assume it's still doing its job.

One hack, out of thirty

This is hack 5 in a series that keeps growing, because there's always one more piece of the day, or the site, quietly worth automating. If a midnight visitor has ever shown up on your site with a question and left without a trace, this is the smallest fix I know for it: five answers, one email field, and a site that's finally awake even when you're not.
