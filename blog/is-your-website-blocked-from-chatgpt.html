Is Your Website Blocked From ChatGPT? A 10-Minute Check for Business Owners

Direct Answer

Your website could be quietly blocked from ChatGPT, and your stats would never show it. A safety check inside ChatGPT can stop it from opening certain sites. If your site gets caught by it, ChatGPT can't read your pages, and nothing shows up in your website stats. It looks rare, but it takes about 10 minutes to check, so it is worth doing once.

What happened

In late October 2026, Search Engine Journal published a report by Dan Taylor about a safety check inside ChatGPT. He found it by watching what ChatGPT does behind the scenes. Inside OpenAI's code, the check goes by the name "Aura."

Here is how it works, in plain words. Before ChatGPT opens a website for someone, it asks a central list: is this site safe to visit? If the answer is no, ChatGPT refuses to open the page. OpenAI's help pages say it may block sites for security, safety and compliance reasons, and its documentation mentions sites linked to illegal activity.

The problem is that a normal, trusted business can end up blocked too. The report describes a large media company whose pages ChatGPT kept refusing to open since May 2026. Its competitors' sites opened fine in the same session. Weeks later, all five pages they tested were still blocked.

One detail stood out to me. The block ignored the user's own settings. Even when someone told their browser to always allow the site, ChatGPT still refused to open it. So this isn't something a visitor can fix by changing a setting. It has to be sorted out at the source.

Why this matters to a small business

Many people now ask ChatGPT questions like "who is a good accountant near me?" or "what does this company offer?" When ChatGPT answers, it often opens websites to read them first.

If ChatGPT can't open your site, three things happen:

  • It can't read your own words. The report notes that ChatGPT may then rely on what other websites say about you instead.
  • Your competitors fill the gap. In the report, rival sites opened fine in the same chat. When ChatGPT can't open your page, it can move on to one it can, and the customer may never know you were an option.
  • You won't see it in your stats. The blocked visit never reaches your website, so your analytics and your web host's logs show nothing at all. You could lose visibility for months without knowing.

A few things are still unknown. Nobody outside OpenAI knows exactly what triggers a block. OpenAI doesn't publish a public appeal process or a timeline for fixes. And the report documents only one case in detail. So I don't think this is a reason to panic. I do think it's a reason to check.

How to check your own site (about 10 minutes)

  1. Ask ChatGPT to open your homepage. Paste your full web address and ask it to open the page and quote the main heading word for word. If it quotes your actual heading, that page opens fine. (A general summary isn't enough, because ChatGPT can sometimes describe a site from search results without opening it.)
  2. Try one more page. Do the same with an important inner page, like your services or booking page.
  3. Try a competitor at the same time. Ask ChatGPT to open a similar business's website. If theirs opens and yours doesn't, that is a useful clue.
  4. Write down what you see. If ChatGPT refuses, copy the exact message and note the date and time. The report lists short error messages, such as one saying the URL cannot be opened.
  5. Rule out your own settings first. Ask your web person to check your security plugin and firewall (the tools that block bad visitors) and your robots.txt (a small file that tells robots which pages they may visit). Sometimes the block is on your side, and that is the easiest fix.
  6. If your settings are fine, contact OpenAI. Open a support case, attach your notes, and keep the case number. In the report, OpenAI support confirmed in writing that a safety block was active and passed the case to a specialist team.
  7. Don't try tricks to get around it. The report notes that OpenAI's rules prohibit bypassing its safety checks. Work through support instead.
  8. Check again every month. Put a 10-minute reminder in your calendar. Things change, and a monthly check means a problem won't go unnoticed for more than a month.

What I'm doing about it

I spend a lot of my week making sure the businesses I work with can be found and quoted by tools like ChatGPT. Until now, my checks focused on what the AI tools say about a business. After reading this report, "can ChatGPT open the site at all?" is now the first item on my list, before anything else.

It's a good reminder of something I tell clients often: being found online isn't one setting you switch on. It's a small set of checks you repeat, and each one takes minutes, not days. If you want the bigger picture on being found by AI tools, I wrote about it here: How to get cited by AI search.
