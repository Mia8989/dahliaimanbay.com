The 15-question check

When customers ask who to hire, is your business named?

A free check for owners and leads of small businesses, nonprofits and solo practices. Ask the questions your customers ask, and see who gets the answer.

Start the check About 30 minutes. Free.
15

questions, in three stages

  • 5 Early: they are learning
  • 5 Comparing: they are choosing
  • 5 Ready: they want to buy

Customers now ask chat assistants and Google’s AI answers who to hire. Some of those answers name a business. The question is whether it is yours.

You would never see this happen on your own. This check shows you. You write 15 questions, ask them in a few free tools, and write down who gets named. Then you fix what keeps you out.

1
Step 1

Write 15 questions in your customer’s words

Write 5 questions for each stage of buying. Fill in the blanks with your own service, place and customer.

Early

They have a problem and are learning about it.

  • What is ?
  • Why does happen?

A bakery might write: Why does a cake sink in the middle?

Comparing

They know what they need and are weighing options.

  • vs
  • Best way to
  • How do I choose a ?

A bakery might write: How do I choose a wedding cake baker?

Ready

They want to buy, book or sign up now.

  • Who offers near ?
  • How much does cost?
  • How do I sign up for ?

A bakery might write: Who makes custom birthday cakes near Logan Square?

TipUse the words customers use on calls and in email, not the words on your website. “Who does bookkeeping for nonprofits near me?” beats “financial services.”

2
Step 2

Ask each question in 2 or 3 free assistants

Use free chat assistants and Google’s AI answers. Places to check include ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google. We are not recommending one. Check the places your customers use.

  1. Open a private or incognito window. This keeps your own past searches from changing the answer.
  2. Paste the question exactly as you wrote it. Do not add your business name.
  3. Read the whole answer. Note every business or website it names.
  4. Write it down right away in the tracker below, before you ask the next question.

4
Step 4

Not named? Start with these four fixes

Look at the rows where you wrote “no.” These four fixes are the quickest place to start.

  1. a

    Make sure your website is not locked

    Your website has a small file called robots.txt. It tells automated visitors, like search engines and assistants, which pages to skip. Type your website address followed by /robots.txt, for example yoursite.com/robots.txt, and read it.

    User-agent: *
    Disallow: /

    If you see these two lines together, every automated visitor is told to stay out. Ask whoever manages your website to change it.

  2. b

    Put key facts on the page as text

    Prices, service areas, hours and how to sign up should be written on the page itself. If they only live in a PDF, a pop-up or behind a login, a tool may never read them.

  3. c

    Answer first, story second

    At the top of the page, answer the customer’s question in 2 to 3 plain sentences. Put your history, details and proof below it.

    A plumber might start with: “Yes, we fix burst pipes the same day in these neighborhoods. Call before noon.” Then tell the story.

  4. d

    Add a fair comparison or “how to choose” page

    Pick a title a customer would type, like “X vs Y” or “How to choose a [your service].” List the 3 things that matter most to the buyer. Be fair, and say who each option suits, including when you are not the best fit.

5
Step 5

Ask the same questions every month

One check tells you little. The same check every month shows you whether your fixes are working.

  1. Use the exact same 15 questions. If you change the words, you cannot compare the results.
  2. Pick the same day each month, like the first Monday, and put it on your calendar.
  3. Use a fresh tracker each time and keep the old ones, so you can see the change.
