AI & Automation

Hacks 1 to 8

1

The 3-email auto-reply

Find the three questions you answer most (pricing, hours, location), write one clean reply for each, save them as Gmail templates, then add a filter so the right reply sends itself.

2

One post = a week of content

Take one published post: the headline becomes a hook, three subheads become three short videos, one stat becomes a graphic, one quote becomes a LinkedIn post, the intro becomes an email.

3

AI writes your first draft, not your final

Give AI your rough notes and ask for a messy first draft, then cut it, add your real story, and fix the voice. It gets you to 60% in two minutes so you spend your energy on the part that sounds like you.

4

The meeting-notes hack

Run a meeting recorder on your calls. It transcribes, summarizes, and pulls out the action items automatically, so you can actually be present instead of typing.

5

The 5-answer chatbot

Load your five most-asked questions into a simple chatbot on your site. It answers instantly, captures the email, and hands you a warm lead in the morning.

6

AI call prep

Five minutes before a big call, drop the person's bio and your notes into AI and ask for three talking points and two smart questions.

7

Automate review requests

Set a trigger that sends a short thank-you and a review link a day after the job is done, right when the client is thrilled.

8

The AI tool audit

Once a quarter, list every subscription and what it actually does. Cancel the overlaps, keep what your team actually opens.

Time & Operations

Hacks 9 to 15

9

Batch by task type

Group similar work into themed days (Monday admin, Tuesday content) instead of jumping between ten kinds of tasks. Task-switching has a real cost.

10

The two-minute rule

If it takes under two minutes, do it right now. Anything longer gets scheduled. One habit that keeps small things from piling into a wall of overwhelm.

11

The "ask me this a lot" folder

Every time you write a genuinely good answer to a client or donor, paste it into one document. In a month you have a library you can copy, tweak, and send in seconds.

12

Time-block your calendar

Block deep work like a meeting you can't move. An empty calendar isn't free time, it's unprotected time.

13

The Friday 15-minute reset

Before you log off Friday, clear your desktop, write your top three tasks for Monday, and close your open loops.

14

Write the SOP the second time

First time you're figuring a task out. Second time, write the steps as you go, so a new hire or a VA can do it without you.

15

Delegate the decision

Give people the goal, the guardrails, and the authority to decide inside them, not just the task. If every choice routes back to you, you have hired hands, not help.

Marketing & Social

Hacks 16 to 22

16

Bio link = front door

Turn your one link into a simple landing page with your top five actions: book a call, read the latest, shop, donate, subscribe.

17

Post the behind-the-scenes

People follow people, not logos. Show the setup, the fix, the real moment. Authentic beats produced.

18

Every question = a video

Every question a customer emails you is your next piece of content. Read it, answer it in 30 seconds, done.

19

The one-email newsletter

Keep it to one short monthly email: a story, a number, a thank you, and one link. Short and consistent beats long and rare.

20

Repost your winners

Only a fraction of your audience saw a post the first time. Refresh the hook and post it again instead of always making something new.

21

Write to one person

Picture one real customer or donor and write to them using "you," not "everyone." Broad messaging is forgettable messaging.

22

Result first, process second

Lead with the outcome, the finished result, the number, then walk through how. Put the payoff in the first two seconds, not the last.

Money, Grants & Growth

Hacks 23 to 30

23

Track every "no"

Log every no with the date and reason, then follow up in 90 days. Budgets and timing change, and you're the one who remembered.

24

Raise prices without apologizing

Lead with the value and the reason, not an apology: "Starting in [month], here's what's included and the new rate."

25

The grant one-pager

Build one master document with your mission, impact numbers, budget, and story ready to copy, so when a grant opens you're assembling, not writing from zero.

26

Know your one number

Pick the one metric that matters most this quarter and put it where you see it daily. Chase every number and you move none of them.

27

Ask for the referral

Go to your five happiest customers or donors and ask directly if they know one person like them who would benefit. It needs a nudge, not just hope.

28

Sell the small first step

Offer a small paid first step (a plan, an audit, a pilot) instead of leading with a big proposal. It lowers the risk and builds trust toward the bigger yes.

29

Read your bank statement

Read it line by line, monthly. Forgotten subscriptions and creeping fees add up; twenty minutes of looking often beats a week of hustling for new revenue.

30

Turn projects into systems

When you finish a job, ask what part could become a template, checklist, or automation for next time.
